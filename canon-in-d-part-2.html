Canon in D, Part 2 — The Melody — on the 0700-AAM

Part 1 built the loop: drums, chords, bass and sparkle — the Canon accompaniment, played in live one layer at a time. But the loop so far is only the accompaniment. Part 2 adds the part everyone hums: the melody, played in over the top of the Part 1 loop while it runs.

Follow this after Part 1, with the Part 1 loop playing.

The harmony underneath never changes — eight chords, one every two steps of the 16-step loop:

D — A — Bm — F#m — G — D — G — A

Step 0 — Start from the Part 1 loop

  1. Build the Part 1 loop first (see the Part 1 tutorial): tempo tapped in at about 45 BPM, drums programmed in the step sequencer, chords captured with OVERDUB, then the bass, then the arpeggio pings.
  2. Press PLAY and let the accompaniment loop run. Everything in Part 2 is played in over this loop, while it plays.

Step 1 — Make the pads a melody keyboard (16 LEVEL keygroup)

The melody sits higher than the chord keygroup reaches, so it gets its own keygroup:

  1. With OVERDUB off, strike pad 7 (the G♯4 hook pad), then press 16 LEVEL. All sixteen pads now play that sound chromatically, source pitch (G♯4) on pad 5. This is the only keygroup on the machine that reaches from E4 all the way up to F♯5.
  2. On this keygroup the pads span E4 to G5, and the math is: pad number = note − 63.

Step 2 — Capture phrase 1 (OVERDUB)

  1. Accompaniment playing, press OVERDUB — its light comes on. With OVERDUB lit, every note you play is written into the loop.
  2. Phrase 1 is long, singing notes — each one lasts 4 steps. Play one note on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13 of the cycle. The phrase takes two full cycles:

  1. The loop comes around playing phrase 1 back over the accompaniment. Keep OVERDUB lit — phrase 2 goes into the next cycles of the same loop.

Step 3 — Capture phrase 2 (OVERDUB still lit)

Phrase 2 moves twice as fast — quarter notes, each lasting 2 steps. Play one note on steps 1, 5, 9 and 13, over the next two cycles:

Step 4 — Let it play, then end it

Let the full loop run — drums, chords, bass and sparkle from Part 1, with the melody riding on top, every part played in by hand. To finish: stop the loop and strike a final D chord (D3 in the bass + D4 F♯4 A4 on the melodic pads) and let it ring.

The Canon is complete: accompaniment and melody together, built entirely by looping.
