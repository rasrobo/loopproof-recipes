House of the Rising Sun — on the 0700-AAM

A complete, followable tutorial. Every step uses the machine's real controls, in order. Follow it top to bottom and you will have the song looping on the machine, built one part at a time — the same way the video of this tutorial was made.

The song: House of the Rising Sun, in A minor, 6/8 feel. The harmony loop is eight chords, one bar each:

Am — C — D — F — Am — E — Am — E

Step 0 — Plug in a keyboard

A keyboard plugs into the machine (USB / MIDI in). Whatever you play, the machine hears. The melodic parts below are played on the keys (or on the machine's pads once the keygroup is set — Step 2); the machine captures them into its loop.

Step 1 — Set the tempo and program the drums

  1. Tap the tempo in on TAP TEMPO — a slow, swaying pulse (the song sits around a dotted-quarter beat of ~56).
  2. Open the step sequencer and click in the drum pattern: - Kick on steps 1 and 9 - Snare on steps 5 and 13 - Hats on every even step
  3. Press PLAY. The drums loop. This loop is the spine of the song — everything else is played over it, one part at a time.

Step 2 — Make the pads a keyboard (16 LEVEL keygroup)

  1. Strike the melodic pad (pad 9) once so the machine knows the sound.
  2. Press 16 LEVEL. All sixteen pads now play that one sound at different pitches, like a keyboard: the source pitch sits on pad 5, and the pads run chromatically from there. On this keygroup, a note's pad is: pad = note − 56 (A3 is pad 1, C4 is pad 4, E4 is pad 8, A4 is pad 13, C5 is pad 16).

Step 3 — Capture the chords (OVERDUB)

  1. With the drums playing, press OVERDUB. Its light comes on. With OVERDUB lit, every note you play is written into the loop — the machine captures the performance. (REC works the same way; OVERDUB is the one you use once something is already looping.)
  2. Play one chord per bar, as block triads, in time with the loop:
Bar Chord Notes Pads
1 Am A3 C4 E4 1 · 4 · 8
2 C C4 E4 G4 4 · 8 · 11
3 D D4 F#4 A4 6 · 10 · 13
4 F A3 C4 F4 1 · 4 · 9
5 Am A3 C4 E4 1 · 4 · 8
6 E B3 E4 G#4 3 · 8 · 12
7 Am A3 C4 E4 1 · 4 · 8
8 E B3 E4 G#4 3 · 8 · 12
  1. When the loop comes around, your chords are playing back — the machine took them. Hands off; let it loop once through.

Step 4 — Capture the bass

  1. Switch the keygroup to the bass pad (pad 13): strike it, press 16 LEVEL. On this keygroup, pad = note − 37 (E2 is pad 3, F2 pad 4, A2 pad 8, C3 pad 11, D3 pad 13).
  2. OVERDUB still lit, play one root per bar on the first beat, and the fifth of the chord on the second beat:

Am → A2 (pad 8), then E3 · C → C3 (pad 11), then G3 · D → D3 (pad 13), then A3 · F → F2 (pad 4), then C3 · Am → A2, E3 · E → E2 (pad 3), then B2 · Am → A2, E3 · E → E2, B2

  1. The loop now carries drums, chords and bass.

Step 5 — Capture the picking (the 6/8 arpeggio)

  1. Back on the melodic keygroup (strike pad 9, 16 LEVEL).
  2. For each chord, roll the figure root – third – fifth – high root – fifth – third, six even strokes per bar. For the D chord, where the high root sits above the pads, repeat the fifth instead.

Am: 57 60 64 69 64 60 · C: 60 64 67 72 67 64 · D: 62 66 69 69 69 66 · F: 57 60 65 69 65 60 · Am: 57 60 64 69 64 60 · E: 59 64 68 71 68 64 · Am: 57 60 64 69 64 60 · E: 59 64 68 71 68 64 (numbers are the notes; pads are note − 56)

  1. This rolling figure is the heartbeat of the song. Once the loop closes, it plays itself.

Step 6 — Capture the melody

OVERDUB still lit, play the line once, over the top:

E4 (pickup) | A4 – B4 | C5 – E5 | D5 – A4 | C5 – E5 | A4 (held) | E4 (held) | A4 (whole bar) | E4 (whole bar)

Step 7 — Let it play, then end it

Let the full loop run — drums, chords, bass, picking and melody, every part played in by hand and captured by the machine. To finish, let the last loop play out and strike a final A minor chord (A2 + A3 C4 E4 A4) and let it ring.

Every part in this song was played in live, one layer at a time, and looped on this machine. Simulator: https://rasrobo.github.io/0700-aam-sim/ — an independent simulator; not affiliated with Music Tribe / Behringer.
