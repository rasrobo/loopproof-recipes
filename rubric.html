LoopProof — Layer & Separation Rubric

The rules every LoopProof arrangement is built and checked against. Written 2026-11-23, after the Canon Part 2 melody-inaudibility fix and the Für Elise audit. A song that fails a measurable rule here does not ship until the rule passes or the deviation is justified in writing in the song's tutorial.

Layer order

Default recording order: drums (programmed) → bass → chords/figures → melody last. Melody is always last and always on its own channel and its own lead voice.

Justified deviation (Für Elise): chords before bass — the bass roots answer the held chords on the third eighth, so the harmony is the frame the bass hangs on. The deviation and its reason are stated in the tutorial.

Per-layer assignment

Every layer gets a register band, a timbre family, and a rhythmic role before anything is recorded. Für Elise (worked example):

Layer Register Timbre Rhythmic role
Drums — kit (kick/snare/hat) the pulse: beats, off-beats, every other step
Chords mid (triads around C4) piano one triad per bar — the harmonic frame
Bass low (A2/E2) bass keygroup one root per held-note bar, on the third eighth
Broken chords low→high climb (E3–B4) piano the written answer figure under each held note
Melody high (A4–E5) flute lead (GM 73) the line itself: figure, held notes, close

Separation gate (measurable)

The lead layer must be audibly above the accompaniment in its own pitch band, measured on the rendered audio with the output audit's method: for each lead note, band RMS in the note's head window vs the median of same-band control windows at other times (excluding windows where the same pitch sounds).

  • Pass: every lead note ≥ +6 dB (presence ratio ≥ 2.0), and the median ≥ +10 dB.
  • Für Elise, measured 2026-11-23: median +18.3 dB, p10 +13.9 dB, min +8.6 dB — PASS.
  • A lead layer rendered in the same voice as its accompaniment at a similar level fails this in practice even when the notes are correct (Canon Part 2, 2026-11-23: present but inaudible). Give the melody a distinct timbre and a velocity lift (~105–112) by default.

Timing & capture facts (simulator)

  • The simulator writes each pad strike into the step that is playing when the pad is hit (state.currentStep). Timing inside a step does not matter; a strike in the neighbouring step is written there. There is no sub-step drift to correct.
  • Passes start on the loop boundary (step 0). The performance's strike log is the ground truth for the strike-completeness gate: every strike of every pass must fall inside the video segment that shows that pass.
  • Tempo is stated in tutorials in display terms (what the machine's display reads while tapping) with a tolerance window, plus the musical tempo it corresponds to.

Dynamics

  • Source dynamics are used when the verified source encodes them. When it doesn't (flat-velocity sources), the dynamics layer is editorial, is labelled as such in the tutorial, and is taught as a physical gesture a learner can reproduce ("press harder over the last three notes"), never only as numbers.
  • The output audit measures the claimed dynamics in the rendered audio (e.g. closing/opening band level ≥ 1.05 for a crescendo).
