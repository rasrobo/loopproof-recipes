LoopProof — The Looping Principles

LoopProof videos teach songs, but the songs are vehicles: what they really teach is how to loop music. Six principles are spoken in every video, each at the moment a step demonstrates it. They are collected here so a learner can read them away from the machine.

  1. Start with a pulse you can trust. Every layer you add later leans on the beat. If the drums wobble, everything wobbles — get the pulse right before anything else exists.
  2. One layer, one job. Drums keep time. Bass gives roots. Chords hold the harmony. The melody tells the story. When a layer tries to do two jobs, the loop turns to mud.
  3. Leave space. A loop repeats forever, so every note you play is a note you'll hear forever. One note in the right place beats four nervous ones.
  4. Listen before you add. Let the loop come around at least once before adding a layer, and only play what you actually hear missing. Looping is listening, then answering.
  5. The melody goes on last and on top. Give it a voice of its own and a level of its own, so the ear always knows where the story is. (Measured, not hoped for: the lead must clear the accompaniment in its own band — see the rubric.)
  6. A good loop sounds finished at any moment. If it only works when it's done, it doesn't work. Build so that every stage is already music — then stopping the capture is an ending, not an interruption.

And one practical corollary the videos demonstrate rather than number: know your grid. On the Kick, the machine writes each strike into the step that is playing when the pad is hit. Timing inside a step doesn't matter; a strike in the neighbouring step is written there. When a note lands one step off, that's why — clear it in the grid view and play it again on the right step.
